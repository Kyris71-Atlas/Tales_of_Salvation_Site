# Stay Close

**Leaf:** 03  
**Session:** 07 — August 19, 2026  
**Status:** On the virtual tome (`deep-memory/witness-open.html` · page 3)  
**Source:** Selective Witness prose from SCR-07 (SCR remains internal; not published)

---

Sol arrives late and is immediately thrown into danger. The party is already deeper in motion than he is, which fits the Witness more honestly than any neat beginning: he enters an unfolding story rather than controlling its first page. Before he fully lands in the moment, the others catch him up on something that changes the road ahead—another creature, besides Jarook, wants the same phylactery. Ezzit’s prize is no longer a single bargain. It is contested.

What follows is the kind of Undermountain hour that piles wonders until they stop feeling separate. There is an altar and statue bound to Ghaunadaur, the Elder Eye. A great eye-shaped mechanism clicks into place. A portal opens and gold begins to fall. The statue wakes and strikes—first blood a natural twenty. Later the party descends another level, closer to Ezzit’s territory. Eli finds a dark presence marked by a ghostly skull. Sol and Iridium help tear that hazard apart. A huge stone creature rises, and the cavern becomes a battlefield of stone, thunder, and badly timed miracles.

In the middle of gods and guardians, something quieter happens. Rupert asks Sol to stay close—explicitly, as Sol has never heard him ask before. Sol chooses to honor it. Deep Memory may care less for the portal’s gold than for that change in distance: a companion closing a gap that used to be empty. Shared light against the necrotic presence matters the same way. Not every memory worth keeping is the largest monster in the room.

Then the table remembers it is a table. Sol’s Eldritch Blast finds a third natural twenty. Eli and Portia answer almost at once with natural ones. Iridium ends the stone creature by putting a thunder-gauntleted uppercut through the problem until the head comes free. The Deep Memory does not need those beats to explain cosmology. It needs them so the night stays human.

When the dust settles, Sol carries a Bead of Force and a harder-earned measure of strength, and the party stands one level deeper than before. Ahead still waits Ezzit—and now the phylactery draws more than one set of eyes. The Memory does not explain why yet. It only keeps what the Witness brought home: contested treasure, a god of outcasts, a request to stay close, and the sound of friends laughing at the same impossible dice.
