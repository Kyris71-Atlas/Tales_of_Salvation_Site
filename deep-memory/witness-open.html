Story of the Witness — so far

First leaf · journal

No one at the table ever truly knew where Sol came from. He appeared carrying more questions than answers, a warlock whose power came from an entity known only as the Deep Memory. Unlike other patrons that demanded worship or promised ambition, the Deep Memory whispered in fragments—echoes of forgotten truths, warnings without explanations, and reminders of events that seemed older than history itself. Sol himself often understood little more than those around him, and that uncertainty became part of his identity. He walked the world not as someone seeking power, but as someone quietly searching for the meaning behind the voice that had chosen him.

As the journey descended into the depths of Undermountain and eventually the shadowed halls of Vanrakdoom, the relationship between Sol and the Deep Memory began to reveal its true nature. The whispers were never commands to conquer, but invitations to perceive differently: count choices instead of enemies, respect forgotten places, do not mistake fear for victory. While companions saw monsters and battles, Sol increasingly saw echoes of forgotten histories and wounds left upon the world. His patron seemed less interested in victory than in remembrance, as though preserving meaning was more important than winning any single fight.

Within the Temple of Shar and the corrupted halls of Vanrakdoom, Sol witnessed both terrible darkness and extraordinary redemption. Vampires long trapped by curses met final peace beneath the light of a Sun Blade. Cultists unknowingly sustained ancient memories that had twisted into nightmares. A bronze dragon thought forever lost to shadow was restored to himself, proving that corruption need not be the end of every story. Throughout these trials Sol remained at the edge of events—rarely the loudest hero, but often the one whose perspective allowed the party to understand what they were truly facing.

Yet for all that was revealed, Sol himself remained a mystery. The Deep Memory hinted at a relationship that predated the pact itself, whispering, “I remember you… You do not remember me.” Those words transformed the patron from a source of magical power into an unresolved question at the heart of Sol’s existence. Whether the Deep Memory was preserving Sol, guiding him toward a forgotten destiny, or recovering something that had once been lost between them remained unanswered. Every revelation seemed only to deepen the mystery rather than resolve it.

The last chapters currently known leave Sol standing not at the end of his journey, but at the edge of a greater one. The redemption of Glister, the unraveling of Vanrakdoom’s ancient tragedy, and the whispers of the Deep Memory have shown that memory itself can shape the fate of worlds. Sol has become more than a warlock bound to an unknowable patron; he has become a traveler between forgotten histories and living choices. His greatest battles may still lie ahead—not against monsters alone, but against the forgotten truths hidden within his own past. Until those memories are finally reclaimed, his story remains unfinished, carried forward one whisper at a time.

The Memory That Traveled

Leaf 02 · Session 06 · August 10, 2026

Sol began this memory inside the Nautiloid, though the deeper they traveled into it, the less certain that name seemed. Wooden furniture and ordinary crew quarters sat where alien architecture had been expected. A spellbook waited without a wizard to read it. A blood-stained chair stood shackled in a room that might once have belonged to a captain. On the bridge, Sol recognized the empty housings where control panels should have been, as though someone had taken away the ship’s hands before abandoning the body. A mind flayer came to reclaim—or defend—what remained, but Portia brought it down before Sol and she needed the escape they had already begun planning silently between them. By the time the party left the vessel, Sol knew less about what it truly was than when he had entered it.

Beyond the ship waited another world beneath the world. Ooze from a place called the Swamp of Oblivion slowed the ground beneath their feet. Gargoyles stood where Sol had hoped to find shelter among crystal statues. Beholders watched—or did not watch—from distant reaches of the cavern, while lesser floating eyes allowed the travelers to pass until one was destroyed and burst open something stranger than flesh. Jarook rose singing from golden sand in a chamber like the hollow heart of a geode, declaring himself master of stone, wealth, and cavern before revealing that even such a creature could be trapped. His bargain pointed deeper still: descend to the next level, destroy Ezzit, and return with the lich’s phylactery. Sol remembered the name. Ezzit had once studied beneath Halaster, had been discarded, and had perhaps answered rejection by teaching himself how not to die.

Then a memory crossed between minds. Portia was caught in the death of the floating eye and saw through a perspective that was not her own: a beholder in the Underdark reducing a rival to dust, another pursuing its quarry through a forest of immense fungi. Sol did not see those moments with his own eyes. He knew them through Portia, carried across the quiet bond already joining their thoughts. Her experience became part of his without becoming his possession. And when Sol carried that borrowed vision inward, the Deep Memory received it farther removed still: beholder to Portia, Portia to Sol, Sol to Memory. A memory of a memory of a memory. Perhaps truth is not weakened merely because it has traveled. Perhaps some truths survive precisely because someone else agrees to carry them.

Not everything worth carrying was ancient or terrible. Somewhere aboard the strange ship, a hamster moved between rooms through a hole in the wall and spoke to Eli in a language or manner Sol could not explain. Clive, confronted with this mystery, responded by casting Greater Restoration upon it. Whatever affliction the creature might once have suffered, it left that encounter in exceptionally good health. Later, Clive flew alone through the cavern to scout what lay ahead. Sol knew there were other ways the party might have looked, other eyes they might have used, but chose not to interrupt the moment. Some memories endure because they reveal great secrets. Others deserve to remain simply because, for a little while, everyone at the table was alive inside them.

The road now continues downward. Behind Sol lies a ship whose true history remains uncertain, a dao who sings of mastery while unable to leave his own cavern, and memories that can apparently leap from one mind into another. Ahead waits Ezzit, discarded student of Halaster, rumored lich, and holder of a phylactery Jarook wants badly enough to send strangers after it. The Deep Memory does not yet say what connects these things. It has learned patience over ages Sol cannot remember. For now, it keeps what the Witness carried to it: a vessel missing its hands, a vision that traveled through three minds, a perfectly healthy hamster, and the name of a man who may have refused to remain discarded.
